# Glossary

Quick definitions for the terms used across these handbooks. New mods: skim this once, then use it as a reference.

## Ban

Removing a user from the server permanently (or long-term). The most severe punishment — reserved for the top of the [punishment ladder](./procedures.md). Always announced at the appropriate level of detail; see [Informing Members of Punishments](./informing-members.md).

## Case number

The unique ID assigned to each entry in a user's modlog. Always include it when referencing a past punishment — e.g. when requesting [modlog edits](./mod-commands.md).

## Escalation

Moving a situation to someone with more authority, or moving a punishment to the next category on the [punishment ladder](./procedures.md). Examples: handing a case to your supervisor, or escalating an unacknowledged timeout to a longer timeout or ban.

## Evidence

Screenshots of punished interactions, saved in `#evidence-for-logs` with the user's ID. Never post evidence in public channels.

## Formal warning

A logged warning issued with `/warn`. Stronger than an informal warning — the user must acknowledge it, and it counts toward escalation on the punishment ladder.

## Harassment

Targeted, repeated behavior that intimidates, degrades, or harms another member. Treated seriously and escalated quickly — see [Rules and Guidelines](./rules-and-guidelines.md).

## Head moderator

The senior moderation role. Only a Head Moderator can approve mod-command testing, and certain actions (like editing modlogs) require their sign-off. See the [Channel & Role Directory](./channel-directory.md).

## Informal warning

A light, first-step correction issued with `/infwarn`. Not as severe as a formal warning, but still logged and still requires acknowledgment.

## Kick

Removing a user from the server while allowing them to rejoin immediately (e.g. members under 16, who may return when they turn 16). Less severe than a ban.

## Modlog

The logged history of a user's punishments, viewed with `/modlogs`. Check it before deciding on a punishment — repeat offenses escalate.

## Ping

An @mention notification. Used for event reminders, follow-ups, and getting someone's attention. Don't overuse — pings should mean something.

## Shadow

Observing a senior moderator handle a live ticket or case without acting yourself. Part of [onboarding](./onboarding-checklist.md) — watch first, act later.

## Supervisor

The staff member overseeing other staff. If the person breaking the rules is a staff member, you don't moderate them yourself — you report it to their supervisor.

## Ticket

A private support channel between staff and a member, opened from `#open-a-ticket`. Used for punishments, appeals, and reports. One topic per ticket; only the attending moderator closes or renames their own tickets.

## Timeout

Temporarily restricting a user from interacting in the server for a set duration. Announced in a ticket with the reason and end time; the ticket stays open until the timeout ends. See [Timeout Procedure](./procedures.md#timeout-procedure).

## Trial mod

A new moderator still in their evaluation period. Trial mods have limited permissions — see the [Channel & Role Directory](./channel-directory.md) — and work under supervision until signed off.
