# Rules and Guidelines

## 1. General Rules

1. **Follow the same server rules that you enforce.** You are not, under any circumstance, exempt from the rules.
2. **No improper or unnecessary use of authority or moderation powers.** You are here to keep the server safe, not to push the boundaries of what you may or may not do as a moderator.
3. **Do not actively seek out to cause any level of disruption** in order to then moderate another user.
4. **Do not escalate issues** — simply handle them as necessary, effectively and efficiently.
5. **Do not take over another moderator's case** in any ticket or other channel unless the attending moderator asks or gives permission. This includes closing the ticket.
6. **Do not involve yourself in issues amongst the moderation team.** Seek assistance from the Head Moderator immediately instead.

:::warning Know your headspace

7. **Only moderate when you're in a clear, mature headspace.** If you're age regressed or not in the right mindset, step back and let another mod handle it.
:::

:::tip Stay neutral

8. **Don't moderate cases involving your friends or people you dislike** — let another moderator take care of it to keep things fair.
:::

9. **Even when you're not actively moderating, you still represent the server and the moderation team** — act accordingly at all times.

## 2. Moderation Rules

1. **If you spot something that needs modding, take care of it** (unless you're on leave or unavailable). If you can't handle the situation, contact another member of the moderation team immediately if possible.
2. **Any action you take is at your own discretion, but it must be justified and reasonable** for the situation. For example: no banning someone for sending a selfie — and no second chances for people shouting offensive slurs in a voice chat.
3. **You may not moderate without enough evidence**, or if there is any reasonable doubt that could lead to unfair judgment.
4. **You may not, under any circumstance, moderate other staff members** — including event team staff — without prior consent from the appropriate supervisor. Most cases should be handled by the supervisor, not you.
5. **Use the appropriate channels.** All moderation commands should be run in `#mod-commands`.
6. **Don't show off your mod actions or powers.** The only exception is sharing necessary logs with someone who reported an issue, to show them it's been handled.

:::info Evidence requests

7. **If a user asks to see the evidence against them, you must show it.** But if sharing it could put another user at risk, you need that user's clear permission first.
:::

## 3. Ticket Rules

1. **Remain professional and polite at all times** when responding to tickets, and act in the best interests of the user — unless the situation calls for a different approach.
2. **Do not close a ticket without permission** from the user who opened it.
3. **Always provide a reason when closing a ticket.** Clearly state what happened and the action taken (e.g. *"Reported user warned for DM violation"*).
4. **Do not claim or respond to a ticket you are unsuitable to handle**, such as application tickets.
5. **Do not rename tickets** unless they are ones you opened and it is necessary to do so.
6. **Do not add users to another ticket** unless doing so to privately speak to a user.
7. **Tickets are private.** Never show the contents of a ticket to other users.

## 4. Other Rules

:::tip Supportive, not harsh

Always consider the community you are supporting. Age regressors can be unpredictable and act recklessly. While this is no excuse for breaking rules, their intentions may not have been to cause harm or distress. Sometimes a stern word is all it takes for first-time offenders. **Always be supportive, not unnecessarily harsh.**
:::
